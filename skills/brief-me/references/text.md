# Text: simplified writing with an honest STE boundary

Use this reference for the primary text format. Follow the user's language choice. A Chinese conversation produces Chinese by default, not an unsolicited English translation.

## Language and compliance

ASD-STE100 is a controlled **English** language with writing rules and a dictionary. Short sentences alone do not establish compliance.

| Request | Produce | Describe the result as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chinese or another non-English language | Clear prose in that language, using the principles below | Simplified writing inspired by STE; not an ASD-STE100 compliant text |
| English, normal brief | English drafted toward STE rules, using consistent technical terms | STE-informed draft; full compliance not verified |
| Explicit strict ASD-STE100 | English checked against the available complete official issue, its dictionary, and documented technical vocabulary | State the issue, review performed, and any unresolved deviations |

For a normal Chinese brief, one unobtrusive note is enough: “采用 STE 简化写作原则；中文不属于 ASD-STE100 合规文本。” Avoid repeating it in every section or adding it to other output modes.

For a strict request, identify the standard issue in use. Obtain an authorized copy if available, without bypassing access or licensing. Review approved words for both their permitted meaning and part of speech; validate technical nouns and technical verbs under that issue's rules. If the complete rules or dictionary are unavailable, deliver a useful draft with “full ASD-STE100 compliance unverified” and identify the missing check. Do not call a model self-check or a sentence-length script certification. If strict STE and Chinese are both requested, explain the incompatibility and ask which requirement takes priority; a Chinese adaptation may accompany an English draft if useful.

## Shape the explanation

Start with the result that changes the reader's understanding. Then explain the minimum reasoning needed to trust it. Include produced artifacts and next actions only when they exist. Use headings or lists when they reduce effort, not to fill a template.

- Put one main idea in each sentence and one topic in each paragraph.
- Prefer familiar, concrete words. Define essential terms once and use the same term for the same thing.
- Use active voice when the actor matters. Say who must take each action.
- State conditions before the action they control. Put instructions in their execution order.
- Separate a fact from its interpretation and from a proposed next step.
- Preserve uncertainty and negative results. “The unit checks passed” does not mean “The feature is ready.”
- Keep names, identifiers, commands, and numbers exact. Explain complex names instead of distorting them.

For English drafting, use these Issue 9 rules as a starting point, not a replacement for the standard:

- Keep procedural sentences at 20 words or fewer, and descriptive sentences at 25 words or fewer, using the standard's counting rules.
- Keep descriptive paragraphs to six sentences or fewer and one topic.
- Use one instruction per sentence, with the standard's exceptions for simultaneous actions.
- Keep multi-word nouns to at most three words where the applicable rules require it; define or rewrite complex terminology.
- Use the permitted verb forms and dictionary entries. A spelling ending in `-ing` is not automatically forbidden: approved adjectives and prepositions also exist.

For Chinese, use short natural clauses, explicit subjects where needed, stable terminology, and limited nesting. English word-count limits do not translate into Chinese character limits. Prioritize meaning over a mechanical count.

## Check the actual brief

Compare every material conclusion with the source conversation. Remove repetition; retain the reason, consequence, or limitation the reader needs. Read instructions as if performing them. For strict English, complete the issue-specific lexical and rule review or mark it unverified. A readable draft is useful even when formal conformity remains unverified.
